A Bangalore-based SaaS startup launched their website last month. Within 48 hours, they discovered their contact form wasn't sending emails, their site loaded in 8 seconds on mobile, and Google couldn't index half their pages because of a misconfigured robots.txt file. They lost an estimated ₹2.5 lakh in potential leads during their launch week—the exact time when traffic and attention were highest.

This happens more often than founders admit. The excitement of launching overshadows the discipline of checking. A website launch isn't a single event; it's a sequence of 50+ decisions that either compound into success or collapse into missed opportunities.

This startup website checklist covers everything you need to verify before clicking that publish button. Whether you're launching a D2C brand in Mumbai, a B2B service in Pune, or a tech product in Hyderabad, these 50 items will protect your launch investment.

## Pre-Launch Planning: Audience, Goals, and Success Metrics

Before touching design or code, document three things: who you're building for, what you want them to do, and how you'll measure success. Skipping this step is why most startup websites become expensive digital brochures that generate no business.

### Define Your Primary Audience Segments

Write down 2-3 specific customer profiles. Not vague descriptions like "SMBs in India" but concrete ones: "Manufacturing unit owners in Ludhiana with 50-200 employees looking to automate inventory management." Include their pain points, where they spend time online, and what objections they'll have. This clarity shapes every headline, image, and call-to-action on your site.

### Set Measurable Launch Goals

Avoid goals like "get more traffic." Instead, define: "Generate 50 qualified leads in the first 30 days" or "Achieve 100 demo bookings by month-end." Attach rupee values where possible. If your average deal size is ₹75,000 and your close rate is 20%, each qualified lead is worth ₹15,000. This math justifies your website investment and guides post-launch optimisation priorities.

### Establish Baseline Metrics

Document current numbers if you have an existing site. If this is a fresh launch, research industry benchmarks. Average conversion rates for Indian B2B websites hover around 2-3%, while D2C e-commerce sees 1-2%. Knowing these baselines prevents both panic and overconfidence in your first weeks.

## Design and UX: Mobile-First, Accessibility, Brand Consistency

Design isn't decoration—it's communication. Every colour choice, font size, and button placement either helps or hinders your visitor's journey toward becoming a customer.

### Mobile-First Testing Protocol

76% of Indian internet users access websites primarily through mobile devices. Test your site on actual phones, not just browser emulators. Check on a ₹10,000 Android device with a Jio connection, not just the latest iPhone on WiFi. Verify that buttons are thumb-friendly (minimum 44x44 pixels), text is readable without zooming, and forms are easy to complete on a small screen.

### Accessibility Compliance Checklist

Accessibility isn't optional—it's good business. Ensure all images have descriptive alt text, colour contrast meets WCAG 2.1 AA standards (4.5:1 ratio for body text), and the site is navigable by keyboard alone. Use tools like WAVE or axe DevTools to catch issues. Beyond ethics, accessible sites perform better in search rankings and reach a wider audience.

### Brand Consistency Audit

Review every page for visual consistency. Check that your logo appears correctly sized in headers and footers, brand colours are applied uniformly (use exact hex codes, not approximations), and typography follows your established hierarchy. Inconsistency signals unprofessionalism and erodes trust—particularly damaging for startups without established reputations.

<Callout type="tip">
Create a simple brand checklist document with hex codes, font names, logo usage rules, and approved imagery styles. Share it with anyone touching your website to prevent drift.
</Callout>

## Content: All Pages Written, Proofread, With Primary Keywords

Content is where most startup websites fail. Founders either rush through copywriting or postpone it until launch pressure forces half-baked text onto critical pages.

### Homepage Messaging Clarity

Your homepage has 5 seconds to answer: "What do you do, and why should I care?" Test this with someone outside your company. Show them your homepage for 5 seconds, then ask what your business offers. If they can't answer clearly, rewrite your headline. Avoid jargon like "synergistic solutions" or "next-generation platforms"—speak in terms of customer outcomes.

### Service and Product Page Depth

Each service page needs 800+ words of substantive content. Include what the service involves, who it's for, what results to expect, how your process works, and pricing transparency (at minimum, a starting range). Thin pages with 200 words and stock photos signal that you're not serious about that offering.

### Proofreading and Localisation

Read every page aloud to catch awkward phrasing. Run text through Grammarly or Hemingway Editor. For Indian audiences, ensure pricing is in rupees, contact numbers include country codes, and addresses use familiar location references. A Gurgaon startup writing "10 minutes from the airport" is more useful than "Sector 48, Near IFFCO Chowk" for visitors unfamiliar with the area.

## Technical SEO: Sitemap, Robots.txt, Canonical, Structured Data

Technical SEO creates the foundation for search visibility. Get this wrong, and your beautiful content stays invisible to Google.

### XML Sitemap Generation and Submission

Generate an XML sitemap listing all indexable pages. Most CMS platforms do this automatically, but verify it exists at `yoursite.com/sitemap.xml`. Check that it includes only live pages (no staging URLs, no 404s) and updates automatically when you add content. Submit it to Google Search Console immediately after launch.

### Robots.txt Configuration

Your robots.txt file tells search engines what to crawl. A single wrong line can block your entire site from indexing. Verify that you're not accidentally blocking important directories, your sitemap location is declared, and staging environment rules haven't carried over to production. Test using Google's robots.txt tester in Search Console.

### Canonical URLs and Structured Data

Set canonical URLs on every page to prevent duplicate content issues. Implement structured data (schema markup) for your business type—LocalBusiness for service companies, Product schema for e-commerce, FAQ schema for content pages. Use Google's Rich Results Test to validate your markup before launch.

## Performance: Core Web Vitals Passing, Images Optimised, Fonts Preloaded

Google uses Core Web Vitals as a ranking factor. More importantly, slow sites lose visitors. A 1-second delay in page load reduces conversions by 7%.

### Core Web Vitals Targets

Aim for these thresholds: Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) under 2.5 seconds, First Input Delay (FID) under 100 milliseconds, Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) under 0.1. Test using PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse. Don't just check your homepage—test your highest-traffic pages and any page with forms or interactive elements.

### Image Optimisation Protocol

Convert images to WebP format (30% smaller than JPEG at equivalent quality). Implement lazy loading for images below the fold. Specify width and height attributes to prevent layout shift. Compress aggressively—most hero images should be under 200KB. Use tools like Squoosh or ImageOptim before uploading.

### Font Loading Strategy

Preload critical fonts in your HTML head to prevent flash of unstyled text. Limit font weights and variants—you rarely need more than 3 (regular, medium, bold). Consider system font stacks for body text; they load instantly and look professional. If using Google Fonts, self-host them for better performance and GDPR compliance.

## Security: SSL, Strong Passwords, Firewall, Backup Configured

Security breaches don't just affect large enterprises. Small websites get hacked constantly—often to inject spam links or cryptocurrency miners. Prevention costs far less than recovery.

### SSL Certificate Verification

HTTPS is mandatory. Verify your SSL certificate is installed correctly, covers all subdomains you use, and auto-renews. Test for mixed content warnings (HTTP resources loading on HTTPS pages) using browser developer tools. A padlock icon missing or a "Not Secure" warning will immediately drive visitors away.

### Access Control and Passwords

Change all default passwords. Use unique, complex passwords for hosting, CMS admin, database, and FTP access. Enable two-factor authentication wherever available. Limit admin access to people who genuinely need it. Document these credentials securely (a password manager, not a Google Doc).

### Backup and Recovery Setup

Configure automated daily backups that store copies off-server (cloud storage, not just your hosting account). Test the restoration process before launch—backups are useless if you can't restore from them. Many Indian hosting providers offer backup services, but verify retention periods and recovery procedures.

## Analytics: GA4, Meta Pixel, GSC Verified, Conversion Tracking Live

You can't improve what you don't measure. Analytics setup before launch means you capture data from day one, when attention is highest.

### Google Analytics 4 Configuration

Install GA4 with enhanced measurement enabled. Set up custom events for business-critical actions: form submissions, phone number clicks, WhatsApp button taps, PDF downloads. Create audiences for remarketing (all visitors, engaged visitors, converters). Verify data is flowing correctly using real-time reports.

### Conversion Tracking Setup

Define what a "conversion" means for your business and track it properly. For lead generation sites, track completed form submissions (not just page views of the thank-you page). For e-commerce, implement full purchase tracking with transaction values. Connect to Google Ads and Meta Ads Manager if you plan paid campaigns.

### Search Console Verification

Verify your site in Google Search Console using DNS verification (most reliable method). Submit your sitemap. Request indexing for your most important pages. Set up email alerts for critical issues. This is your direct communication channel with Google—don't skip it.

## Legal: Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Cookie Consent, DPDP Compliance

Legal compliance isn't exciting, but non-compliance is expensive. The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 applies to any business collecting data from Indian users.

### Privacy Policy Requirements

Your privacy policy must clearly state what data you collect, why you collect it, how you store it, who you share it with, and how users can request deletion. Don't copy-paste templates blindly—customise for your actual practices. Include contact information for privacy-related queries. Update the policy date whenever you modify it.

### Cookie Consent Implementation

Implement a cookie consent banner that blocks non-essential cookies until users opt in. "By continuing to browse, you accept cookies" banners don't meet DPDP requirements. Users must take affirmative action to consent. Store consent records in case you need to demonstrate compliance later.

### Terms of Service and Business Information

Include terms governing site use, limitation of liability, and dispute resolution. For businesses: display your registered company name, GST number (if applicable), and physical address. E-commerce sites have additional disclosure requirements under Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020.

## Forms and Integrations: Contact Form Tested, CRM Connected, WhatsApp CTA

Forms are where visitors become leads. A broken form during launch week is money evaporating.

### Form Functionality Testing

Submit test entries through every form on your site. Verify emails arrive in the correct inbox (check spam folders), auto-responders trigger correctly, and form data appears in your CRM or spreadsheet. Test on mobile devices—thumb-sized touch targets, auto-fill compatibility, and validation messages that don't break layout.

### CRM and Email Integration

Connect forms to your CRM (Zoho, HubSpot, Freshsales—whatever you use) and verify lead records create correctly. Set up email notifications for new submissions so they don't sit unseen. Create a simple lead routing process before launch, even if it's just "all leads go to the founder's inbox."

### WhatsApp Business Integration

78% of customer enquiries in India happen through WhatsApp. Add a floating WhatsApp button or include it prominently in your contact options. Use the official WhatsApp Business API link format: `https://wa.me/91XXXXXXXXXX?text=Your%20message`. Test that conversations open correctly on both mobile and desktop.

<Callout type="warn">
Don't rely solely on WhatsApp for lead capture. Always have a backup contact method (email, phone) for users who prefer alternatives or face technical issues.
</Callout>

## Post-Launch: Submit to Search Engines, IndexNow, Announcement Plan

Launching isn't the finish line—it's the starting gun. Your first 72 hours set the trajectory for organic growth.

### Search Engine Submission

Request indexing for your homepage and key pages through Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool. Submit your sitemap to Bing Webmaster Tools (which also feeds Yahoo and DuckDuckGo). If your CMS supports IndexNow protocol, enable it for faster indexing of new content.

### Launch Announcement Strategy

Plan your announcement before launch day. Prepare social media posts, email to existing contacts, and outreach to relevant communities. If you have a Product Hunt listing, coordinate timing. Local startup communities (Delhi NCR Angels, Bangalore Startups WhatsApp groups) can provide early traffic and feedback.

### Monitoring and Quick-Fix Protocol

Monitor your site intensively for the first week. Check analytics daily for unexpected behaviour. Watch Search Console for crawl errors. Have a developer available for quick fixes—the issues that emerge under real traffic are different from staging environment testing.

## Moving From Checklist to Launch

Fifty items sounds overwhelming, but most take under 10 minutes each. The discipline of checking saves the chaos of fixing. Spread this checklist across 2-3 weeks, grouping tasks by expertise needed: marketing team handles content and analytics, developers handle technical SEO and performance, leadership reviews legal and business information.
