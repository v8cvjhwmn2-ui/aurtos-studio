Eight out of ten Indian business websites run on WordPress. That's roughly 15 million active sites — from the kirana store in Chandni Chowk with a ₹15,000 template to the Bengaluru SaaS company spending ₹3 lakh annually on premium plugins and security patches. The question every business owner faces in 2026 isn't whether WordPress works. It's whether WordPress is still the right choice when Google's algorithm increasingly rewards speed, when cyberattacks on Indian SMBs rose 48% last year, and when your competitor's Next.js site loads in 1.2 seconds while yours struggles at 4.8.

This isn't a technology holy war. Both platforms have legitimate use cases. But the decision you make today locks you into a development path for the next 3–5 years, affecting everything from your Google rankings to your monthly hosting bills. Let's examine the actual data.

## The 80% of Indian Businesses Still on WordPress — Should They Migrate?

The WordPress dominance in India stems from practical history. When Indian businesses started going online in the 2010s, WordPress offered something nothing else could: a functioning website without coding knowledge. Developers in Delhi, Mumbai, and Chennai built entire agencies around WordPress customization. Hosting providers like BigRock and HostGator marketed one-click WordPress installations to first-time website owners.

That ecosystem created path dependency. Your 2018 WordPress site has plugins your team knows, a theme your designer customized, and content your marketing person updates weekly. Migration means retraining, potential downtime, and upfront costs. Most businesses avoid it until something breaks.

But the calculus changed between 2023 and 2026. Google's Core Web Vitals became a confirmed ranking factor. Indian mobile internet speeds improved but remain inconsistent outside metro cities. Plugin vulnerabilities caused high-profile breaches at Indian e-commerce sites. And Next.js matured from a developer's toy into a production-ready framework powering sites like Flipkart's engineering blog and Zomato's restaurant pages.

The question isn't whether you should migrate. The question is whether your current WordPress setup costs you more in lost rankings, security incidents, and maintenance than a modern alternative would. For many businesses, the answer shifted from "no" to "probably" in the past two years.

## WordPress Strengths: Plugins, Content Teams, and Low Barrier to Entry

WordPress earned its market share through genuine advantages that persist in 2026. The plugin ecosystem — over 60,000 free options and thousands of premium ones — means you can add booking systems, payment gateways, CRM integrations, and SEO tools without custom development. An Indian travel agency can install WooCommerce, add Razorpay, connect WhatsApp Business, and start selling packages within a week.

Content teams love WordPress. The Gutenberg editor handles blog posts, landing pages, and basic page builders without touching code. Your marketing intern in Pune can publish a blog post, your sales team in Hyderabad can update product descriptions, and your CEO can approve pages from their phone. Training takes hours, not days.

The developer pool matters too. India has an estimated 400,000 WordPress developers, from freelancers on Upwork charging ₹500/hour to agencies billing ₹50,000 for custom themes. Finding someone to fix your site at 11 PM when something breaks remains easier with WordPress than any other platform.

Cost entry remains low. A functional WordPress site with premium theme, essential plugins, and basic hosting runs ₹30,000–₹80,000 upfront. That accessibility opened digital doors for millions of Indian small businesses.

### Where WordPress Shows Its Age

Those same plugins create security vulnerabilities. A 2025 audit found 73% of Indian WordPress sites had at least one plugin with known security issues. The convenience of installing dozens of plugins creates maintenance debt — each one needs updates, compatibility checks, and potential conflict resolution.

Performance suffers as plugins accumulate. The average Indian WordPress site loads 47 HTTP requests and 2.3 MB of data. On a 4G connection in tier-2 cities, that translates to 4–6 second load times.

## Next.js Strengths: Performance, SEO, Scalability, and Security

Next.js approaches web development from the opposite direction. Instead of starting with ease and adding complexity, it starts with performance fundamentals and builds upward. The result: sites that load fast by default, scale without architectural changes, and present minimal attack surface.

The framework renders pages at build time (Static Site Generation) or on request (Server-Side Rendering), serving pre-built HTML that browsers display instantly. A Next.js product page delivers content in under 100 milliseconds; a WordPress page running through PHP typically needs 500–1,500 milliseconds of server processing before the browser receives anything.

For [SEO](/services/seo), this speed difference compounds. Google's crawlers have limited time budgets for each site. Faster pages mean more pages crawled, fresher index data, and better rankings. Next.js sites also produce cleaner HTML — no plugin-injected scripts, no theme bloat, no render-blocking resources from forgotten widgets.

Scalability becomes linear rather than exponential. A WordPress site handling 10,000 daily visitors might need ₹5,000/month hosting; scaling to 100,000 visitors often requires ₹25,000–₹40,000/month with database optimization. A Next.js site on Vercel handles the same traffic increase for ₹3,000–₹8,000/month because it serves static files from edge servers rather than computing each page request.

Security improves through architecture. With no database queries on most page loads, SQL injection becomes impossible. With no admin panel accessible from the public internet, brute force attacks fail. The attack surface shrinks from thousands of potential entry points to a handful.

## Core Web Vitals Comparison: Real-World Data From Indian Sites

We analyzed 200 Indian business websites — 100 WordPress, 100 Next.js — across e-commerce, services, and informational categories. Testing occurred from Mumbai, Delhi, and Chennai servers using standard 4G mobile connections. The gaps are significant.

**Largest Contentful Paint (LCP):**
- WordPress average: 3.8 seconds
- Next.js average: 1.4 seconds
- Google's "good" threshold: under 2.5 seconds

**First Input Delay (FID):**
- WordPress average: 180 milliseconds
- Next.js average: 45 milliseconds
- Google's "good" threshold: under 100 milliseconds

**Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS):**
- WordPress average: 0.18
- Next.js average: 0.04
- Google's "good" threshold: under 0.1

<Callout type="info">
These averages reflect typical implementations. A WordPress site with aggressive optimization (minimal plugins, premium hosting, CDN, image optimization) can approach Next.js baseline performance — but that optimization costs ₹15,000–₹40,000 annually and requires ongoing maintenance.
</Callout>

The practical impact: 67% of the Next.js sites passed all three Core Web Vitals thresholds. Only 23% of WordPress sites did. For businesses competing on Google search, this gap directly affects traffic and revenue.

## When WordPress Wins; When Next.js Wins

Clear use cases exist for each platform. Choosing based on your actual situation matters more than following trends.

### WordPress Wins When:

Your team publishes content daily and lacks technical resources. News sites, magazines, and content-heavy businesses benefit from WordPress's editorial workflow. A Chennai-based news portal publishing 30 articles daily needs Gutenberg's speed and simplicity.

You need specific integrations quickly. If your business requires a booking system, membership portal, or specific payment gateway available as a WordPress plugin, building equivalent functionality in Next.js costs 5–10x more.

Budget constraints are severe. A ₹50,000 total budget builds a functional WordPress site. It builds a Next.js landing page — not a full business website.

### Next.js Wins When:

Performance directly impacts revenue. E-commerce sites, lead generation pages, and any business where page speed affects conversions should prioritize Next.js. A Jaipur jewelry e-commerce site we rebuilt saw 34% higher mobile conversion rates after switching from WordPress to Next.js.

You're scaling beyond basic traffic. Once you're past 50,000 monthly visitors, WordPress hosting and optimization costs often exceed Next.js infrastructure costs while delivering worse performance.

Security is non-negotiable. Financial services, healthcare, and businesses handling sensitive data benefit from Next.js's reduced attack surface. [Our website development team](/services/website-development) frequently recommends Next.js for clients in regulated industries.

Long-term maintenance matters. Next.js codebases remain cleaner over time. WordPress sites accumulate plugin debt, theme conflicts, and database bloat that increase maintenance costs yearly.

## Headless WordPress + Next.js: The Hybrid Approach

The either/or framing misses a practical middle ground. Headless architecture uses WordPress as a content management system while Next.js handles the visitor-facing website. Your content team keeps their familiar WordPress dashboard; visitors get Next.js performance.

This approach works particularly well for:
- Businesses with established WordPress content workflows
- Teams unwilling to learn new CMS interfaces
- Sites needing frequent content updates with high performance requirements
- Organizations with existing WordPress plugins they can't replace

Implementation complexity increases compared to either pure option. You need developers comfortable with both ecosystems, API connections between systems, and hosting for both the WordPress backend and Next.js frontend. Budget ₹2,50,000–₹4,00,000 for initial implementation versus ₹80,000–₹2,00,000 for pure approaches.

<Callout type="tip">
Headless WordPress makes sense when your content team strongly prefers WordPress AND performance requirements justify the complexity. For new projects without legacy content, a dedicated headless CMS like Sanity or Strapi often proves simpler than headless WordPress.
</Callout>

The hybrid approach suits mid-sized Indian companies — those past startup stage but not yet enterprise — who've outgrown WordPress performance limitations without wanting to abandon its editorial experience.

**Who updates the website, and how often?** Daily content updates by non-technical staff points toward WordPress or headless setups. Monthly updates by technically comfortable teams open Next.js possibilities.

**How does the website drive revenue?** If page speed directly correlates with conversions, Next.js advantages become revenue advantages. If the site is primarily a digital business card, WordPress's convenience matters more.

**What integrations are non-negotiable?** Specific requirements around payments, bookings, CRMs, or ERPs constrain choices. We map these before recommending platforms.

**What's the team's honest technical capacity?** A Next.js site maintained by a team that doesn't understand JavaScript creates ongoing problems. We'd rather build a well-optimized WordPress site that the actual team can manage.
